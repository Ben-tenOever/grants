Most of this list is decided in your first two years.

That is the difference between a graduate fellowship calendar and a postdoctoral one. The largest award here can be applied to exactly once, in the first year of the PhD, with a deadline that falls about two months after arriving. Several others close their windows at year two or year three. A student who first looks at this page in year four is reading a much shorter list than a student who looks in the summer before matriculating. Each entry carries the eligibility window, the money, the citizenship rule and the next deadline — computed live in your browser from the recurring pattern, so the dates stay right after this page stops being edited. Terms were verified against primary sources on 22 September 2026.

Looking for something to propose? The companion project ideas page turns this list around: research themes drawn from the departmental lab profiles, with project directions sized for a thesis aim and a fellowship application, each tagged with the awards here that fit it.

Start with the citizenship filter, then the year-in-programme rule. Every federal mechanism on this page — the F31, the F30, the F99/K00, the NSF GRFP, NDSEG, the two DOE programmes — is closed to students on a visa. What is left for an international student is real and worth knowing precisely: the Gilliam is the largest of them, and the disease foundations are markedly more open than the government is.

Two eligibility facts that cost people a cycle. The NSF GRFP no longer accepts second-year graduate students, so an enrolled PhD student realistically gets one attempt, in year one — and MD/PhD students are excluded from it outright. Separately, GRFP's Life Sciences scope now excludes patient-oriented, clinical, epidemiological and disease- or treatment-focused research, which disqualifies a large share of biomedical projects no matter what year the applicant is in. Read the excluded-subject list before the eligibility rules.

Apply before you defend

These are postdoctoral fellowships, which is why they are usually filed under "not yet". But the application happens while you are still a student, and the deadlines sit twelve to eighteen months before the money starts. Treating them as postdoc business is the most common way a senior student loses a year of independent salary support.

Travel, technique and professional development

Small money, low competition, and almost entirely unclaimed. Several of these are effectively automatic for anyone who has already paid society dues and submitted an abstract. They do not substitute for stipend support, but they are what pays for the meeting where the next project starts, or the ten weeks in someone else's lab learning the method the thesis needs.

Discontinued, dormant, or unreachable from here

These still appear on funding lists, university pages and older advice. Several were significant sources of support for exactly the students who can least afford to lose a cycle, and two of them are still being recommended by advisers working from memory.

Ford Foundation Fellowships — all three levels discontinued, not just the postdoctoral one. The final competition across predoctoral, dissertation and postdoctoral tracks was 2023, with a limited set of awards in 2024 and the programme winding down by 2028. The National Academies' Ford pages now return 404. The common belief that the predoctoral and dissertation competitions survived the postdoctoral wind-down is wrong.

NIH F31-Diversity — expired and not reissued. PA-23-271 was archived in February 2025, and when NIH reissued the fellowship family in June 2025 it published an F31, an F32, an F33 and two F30s with no diversity counterpart. NIH issued no explicit discontinuation notice, which is why some institute pages — NIAID's among them — still list it as available. Published analysis counts 405 F31 awards affected by the 2025 terminations, two-thirds of them F31-Diversity.

EPA STAR graduate fellowships — ended. The final cohort was selected under the 2015 solicitation; EPA's current page states the resource is no longer available. What EPA now offers graduate students is an NSF-partnered residency, not a fellowship competition.

Josephine de Kármán — no longer a national award. The trust moved to the Pasadena Community Foundation in 2023, the amount rose to $28,000, and eligibility narrowed to students at eleven named California institutions. Any list describing it as nationally open is out of date, as is the old dekarman.org domain.

Microsoft Research PhD Fellowship — restructured. It is now the Microsoft Research Fellowship: proposal-driven, scoped to named research challenges, open to PhD students, postdocs and faculty alike, and paid as a one-time institutional gift of $17,000 to $47,000 depending on region rather than as multi-year stipend support.

Not national, despite appearances: the Burroughs Wellcome Graduate Diversity Enrichment Program is restricted to PhD students at North Carolina institutions and pays $5,000 across two years; Knight-Hennessy funds only Stanford degrees; and GEM, GFSD and ARCS all require your institution to be a member or partner.

AAI Careers in Immunology Fellowship Program — dormant. The most recent cohort was 2024, the programme page has been removed and now returns a 404, and it no longer appears on AAI's award deadline listing; only the recipient pages survive. Its loss removes the only immunology-specific predoctoral salary mechanism, and leaves AAI offering graduate students the two no-cost trainee meeting awards and nothing else. The AAI–Thermo Fisher Trainee Achievement Award went the same way.

Dormant or unverifiable: the Gates Millennium Scholars programme is closed, and even when it ran, graduate funding was available only to continuing scholars; the UNCF/Merck graduate and dissertation fellowship has had no open cycle since 2014 and no notice of discontinuation; ASM's Robert D. Watkins Graduate Research Fellowship and Raymond W. Sarber Award have no current cycle; the Michelson Philanthropies & Science Prize for Immunology ended with the AAAS partnership; the NVIDIA Graduate Fellowship has posted no 2027–28 cycle; the Quad Fellowship lists future dates as to be announced; and Autism Speaks' Dennis Weatherstone Predoctoral Fellowship has no traceable current cycle.

Not available from a US institution: the Gates Foundation concluded its fellowship and internship programmes in 2021 and funds no graduate research; Wellcome requires a host organisation in the UK, Ireland or an eligible low- or middle-income country, and its eligible list narrows further from October 2026. Neither is a route for a student here.

Method and maintenance

Every entry was checked against the sponsor's own page on 22 September 2026 rather than against aggregators, which are the usual source of stale deadlines and superseded award amounts. Where two sources disagreed, the sponsor's page won — and where the sponsor's own pages disagreed with each other, that is said here rather than quietly resolved.

Deadlines are computed, not typed. Each award stores its recurring pattern — a fixed calendar date, or a rule such as "the second Friday in November" — and the page resolves the next occurrence when you load it. The dates therefore stay correct into future cycles without anyone editing this file. What will drift is the money and the eligibility fine print, so treat the sponsor link as authoritative and this page as the thing that tells you a deadline is coming. Two programmes set their dates freshly in each solicitation rather than following a rule — the NSF GRFP, which moved three weeks earlier this year, and NDSEG — so confirm those two directly.

Three corrections worth stating outright. The HHMI Gilliam is no longer nomination-routed: HHMI now runs an open competition with no institutional cap, and the student and adviser apply as a pair. Ford's predoctoral and dissertation tracks ended alongside the postdoctoral one rather than surviving it. And the widely repeated story that NIH restricted fellowship eligibility for non-citizens in 2025 traces to a four-day typo in five reissued NOFOs, corrected by NOT-OD-25-126 — a correction that ran in the restrictive direction. The standing NRSA rule, unchanged, is citizens, non-citizen nationals and permanent residents.

Left unresolved on purpose. NSF's GRFP solicitation says "less than one academic year completed" while NSF's own FAQ says "not more than one academic year" — they differ exactly at the boundary a second-year applicant cares about, so verify with GRFP programme staff rather than trusting either. The Burroughs Wellcome CASI 2027 cycle is unposted and four BWF pages contradict one another. And the July 2026 DHS rule replacing duration of status with a fixed four-year admission period for F and J students took effect on 15 September 2026 and has been reported to be in litigation; international students should check its operative status before planning a timeline around it.

What is deliberately excluded. One-off calls, programmes with no verifiable current cycle, awards requiring a conferred doctorate or an independent appointment (collected separately above), institutional fellowships that are really recruitment vehicles for a single university, and programmes open only to undergraduates or medical students.

Three structural facts the list makes visible. There is no predoctoral cancer fellowship from any of the major cancer foundations — a striking gap given how much predoctoral cancer research there is. There is now effectively no immunology-specific predoctoral fellowship either: with the AAI programme dormant, students in infection and immunity compete in the open-field and federal pools like everyone else, which is why the filter for that category is nearly empty and should be. And the disease foundations are substantially more open to international students than the federal mechanisms are: every federal award here is closed to visa holders, while most of the foundation awards are not.
