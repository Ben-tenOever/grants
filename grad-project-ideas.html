Thirty-six directions, none of them assignments

A thesis has to be the student's own, and so does a fellowship application — reviewers can tell when a proposal was handed down, and the F31 is scored in large part on whether the work will train the person writing it. What follows is deliberately upstream of both: research themes represented in this department, the techniques already running in the building, and directions that sit adjacent to existing strengths rather than inside them. Find the theme your rotation touched, then argue with the ideas under it. The version you disagree your way into will be better than anything here.

Read this before you pick a direction, if you are in your first year. The NSF GRFP is the one award here that a first-year can win and no one else can, and it now excludes patient-oriented, clinical, epidemiological, and disease- or treatment-focused research from its Life Sciences scope. That is not a formality — it means the same laboratory can host a GRFP-eligible mechanistic project and a GRFP-ineligible translational one. Directions below that can be framed for the GRFP are tagged for it; the ones that cannot are not, and the omission is deliberate rather than an oversight.

How the fellowship tags work. Each direction carries the awards whose scope and eligibility actually fit it — not every award a graduate student could theoretically enter. Deadlines, citizenship rules and year-in-programme windows for all of them are on the graduate fellowships page. Where a direction is tagged NIH F30 rather than F31, it is one that reads particularly well from a dual-degree student; the F31 is available either way.

Ideas that need two labs

These do not belong to any single group, which is exactly why they tend to go unproposed. Each is a thesis-sized question that becomes tractable when two sets of techniques already in this department are put in the same room. Co-mentorship is also a genuinely strong structure on an F31, provided the two mentors write letters that agree with each other about who teaches what — and provided the student can say which lab they will defend from.

Microbiome modelling × MRSA persistence
Does the community around a coloniser predict which one turns invasive?

Longitudinal isolate genomics says what a colonising strain became; ecological modelling of the surrounding community may say why. A thesis here starts descriptive and becomes predictive, which is a structure that survives the inevitable year when the mouse work does not cooperate.

Envelope proteolysis × structural biology
A protease–adaptor complex caught mid-cycle

The genetics and biochemistry of envelope protease activation are worked out here; what is missing is the conformational trajectory. Cryo-EM of trapped intermediates converts a regulatory model into a druggable mechanism, in a WHO priority pathogen, and it teaches a bacterial geneticist structure.

Myeloid biology × respiratory transmission
Does the airway myeloid compartment decide who transmits?

Transmission models measure infectiousness; myeloid genetics can remove one macrophage subset at a time. Together they ask whether an immune cell type, not just viral load, sets how much virus leaves a host — a question neither approach can reach alone.

Translational control × vaccine immunology
Is antibody durability set at the level of translation?

Translation-factor balance decides T cell lineage commitment in culture. Human vaccination cohorts measure durability but rarely mechanism. Testing whether the translational state of a Tfh cell at priming predicts antibody persistence a year later connects the two, and gives an RNA-trained student a legitimate immunology training plan.

Milk and early-life microbiome × neonatal pathogenesis
Where does a newborn's resistome actually come from?

Source-tracking methods can apportion microbial origin between milk, maternal gut and environment. Applied to resistance genes rather than taxa, and paired with neonatal colonisation models, a descriptive observation becomes a mechanism with an intervention point.

Programmable viral vectors × tissue macrophages
Interferon switched off in exactly one cell type

Cell-type-restricted vectors can deliver an immune antagonist to a single population in vivo. Aimed at a defined macrophage subset, this asks which cell's interferon response actually controls disease outcome — something conditional knockouts approximate but never isolate, because they remove the pathway from development onward.

Turning one of these into an F31

Predoctoral applications fail structurally more often than scientifically, and the structural failures are specific and avoidable. Almost none of what follows is about the science.

The F31 is a training grant with a research plan attached, not the reverse. The reviewers are asked whether this candidate, in this environment, with this sponsor, will become an independent scientist. A flawless set of aims attached to a training plan that teaches nothing new scores worse than good aims with a plan that names three techniques the applicant cannot yet do and the person who will teach each one. Every direction on this page was chosen to sit one step outside an existing strength for exactly that reason.

Check the clock before the science. The single largest award a graduate student can win — the NSF GRFP — accepts enrolled students only in the first year, once, with a deadline in October. That means it is planned in the summer before matriculating, not after the first committee meeting. The Hertz closes at year two, the Soros at year two, the Gilliam runs in years two and three, and the F99/K00 wants one to two years left. Work out which windows are still open before choosing what to write.

Citizenship forks the whole list. Every federal mechanism — F31, F30, F99/K00, GRFP, NDSEG, both DOE programmes — is closed to students on a visa. An international student's real list is shorter but not empty, and it is worth establishing which list applies before writing a page. The Gilliam is the largest multi-year award open to international students, and the disease foundations are substantially more open than the government is.

Propose the thing that is adjacent, not identical. A proposal that is a natural continuation of what the lab already publishes reads as a strong preliminary-data section and a weak training plan. The most common review comment on a resubmitted F31 is not that the science was wrong; it is that the candidate could already do all of it.

The sponsor statement is half the application and is the part most often left to the last week. It has to say specifically what the student will learn, from whom, what the lab's funding situation is, and what happens to the project if the student leaves — and for a co-mentored proposal, the two statements must describe the same division of labour. Reviewers notice when they do not.

Where the ideas came from. Themes and available techniques were drawn from the department's own lab research profiles — the same source the grant slate and the postdoctoral ideas page use. Two profiles are left out, the same two the postdoctoral page omits: one lab whose principal investigator has left the institution, and one where a degree running five or six years would outlast the sponsoring lab. No individuals are named anywhere on this page, by design: the themes are meant to be recognisable to the people working in them without reading as an instruction to anyone.
