Forty-five directions, none of them assignments

A fellowship application has to be the applicant's own idea — reviewers can tell, and the strongest applications propose something the sponsor was not already doing. What follows is deliberately upstream of that: research themes represented in this department, the techniques already running in the building, and directions that sit adjacent to existing strengths rather than inside them. Find the theme your work touches, then argue with the ideas under it. The version you disagree your way into will be better than anything here.

One time-sensitive thing worth knowing. The NSF Postdoctoral Research Fellowships in Biology moved to a single focus area this cycle: the intersection of artificial intelligence and the biological sciences. That is an unusually close match for the computational microbiome, AMR-genomics and modelling work in this department, and the award improved to $110,000 a year for 24 months. It closes on the fourth Tuesday of September. Anyone in those areas who is within 15 months of the doctorate should look at it this week.

Ideas that need two labs

These do not belong to any single group, which is exactly why they tend to go unproposed. Each one is a fellowship-sized question that becomes tractable when two sets of techniques already in this department are put in the same room. A co-mentored fellowship application is also a genuinely strong structure with reviewers, provided the two mentors write letters that agree with each other.

Microbiome modelling × MRSA persistence
Can within-host evolution be forecast from the community around the pathogen?

Longitudinal isolate genomics tells you what a colonising strain became. Ecological modelling of the surrounding community may tell you why. Pairing them asks whether the microbial context predicts which coloniser turns invasive — a prediction problem with an unusually clean clinical endpoint.

Structural biology × envelope proteolysis
Structures of a protease–adaptor complex caught mid-cycle

The genetics and biochemistry of envelope protease activation are worked out; what is missing is the conformational trajectory. Cryo-EM of trapped intermediates would convert a regulatory model into a druggable mechanism, and the target is a WHO Priority 1 pathogen.

Macrophage biology × respiratory transmission
Does the airway myeloid compartment determine who transmits?

Transmission models measure infectiousness; myeloid genetics can remove one macrophage subset at a time. The combination asks a question neither can ask alone — whether an immune cell type, not just viral load, sets how much virus leaves a host.

Translational control × vaccine immunology
Is antibody durability set at the level of translation?

Translation-factor balance decides T cell lineage commitment in culture. Human vaccination cohorts measure durability but rarely mechanism. Testing whether the translational state of a Tfh cell at priming predicts antibody persistence a year later connects the two.

Maternal–infant microbiome × neonatal pathogenesis
Where does a newborn's resistome actually come from?

Source-tracking algorithms can quantitatively apportion microbial origin between milk, maternal gut and environment. Applied to resistance genes rather than taxa, and paired with neonatal colonisation models, it turns a descriptive observation into a mechanism with an intervention point.

Programmable viral vectors × tissue macrophages
Interferon signalling switched off in exactly one cell type

Cell-type-restricted vectors can deliver an immune antagonist to a single population in vivo. Applied to a defined macrophage subset, it answers which cell's interferon response actually controls disease outcome — a question conditional knockouts approximate but never quite isolate.

Turning one of these into an application

The mechanics matter more than they should, and most of what sinks a first fellowship application is structural rather than scientific.

Check the clock before the science. The most valuable early-stage award here — four years of salary plus loan repayment — requires basic scientists to apply within 18 months of the degree and with no more than a year in the sponsor's lab. That means it is planned before the PhD ends, not after arriving. Anyone already past that window should go straight to the programmes with five-year eligibility, which are the more forgiving and in one case the broadest award on the list.

Citizenship forks the whole list. The two largest federal sources both require citizenship or permanent residency. A postdoc on a visa has a real but different list, and it is worth establishing which one applies before writing a page. Two programmes stand out for having no citizenship bar at all.

Propose the thing that is adjacent, not identical. Fellowship reviewers are asked whether the applicant is being trained, which means the proposal should require something the applicant cannot already do. A project that is a natural continuation of the thesis reads as a strong preliminary-data section and a weak training plan. Every direction on this page was chosen to sit one step outside an existing strength for that reason.

The sponsor letter is half the application and is the part most often left to the last week. It should say specifically what the applicant will learn, from whom, and what happens to the project afterwards — and for a co-mentored proposal, the two letters must describe the same division of labour. Reviewers notice when they do not.

Where the ideas came from. Themes and available techniques were drawn from the department's own lab research profiles — the same source the grant slate uses. Two groups were left out deliberately: one whose principal investigator has left the institution, and one where a fellowship running three or four years would outlast the sponsoring lab. No individuals are named anywhere on this page, by design: the themes are meant to be recognisable to the people working in them without reading as an instruction to anyone.
